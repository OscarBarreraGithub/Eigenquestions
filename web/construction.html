How the physics atlas is constructed

The aim is a map of questions researchers can recognize: what is being asked, which assumptions matter, how a calculation proceeds, and where related work belongs. Every destination should lead back to sources.

From a numerical proposal to a usable question

  1. Propose neighborhoods. Existing source embeddings and graph connections nominate nearby material and possible refinements.
  2. Challenge them against papers. Compare the physical system, target quantity, regime, conventions and method. Similar language alone cannot settle whether two questions are the same.
  3. Repair scope and overlap. Separate changed premises; retain legitimate shared topics and method connections. Unresolved comparisons and withheld promotions remain visible.
  4. Give a scoped question a shared identity. Reaching it through another parent or a justified paraphrase should recover the same destination. A changed defining assumption may require a different question.
  5. Explore through one browser and agent interface. Inspect sources, choose a direction, branch, return or replay. An agent can orient independently before applying a visible update to the browser.

How the detailed map gets its shape

Existing question embeddings propose useful neighbors. Source review then determines the scoped destinations, their overlaps and the links a researcher can follow. The chart is built after these decisions, balancing papers so one paper's many extracted descriptions do not dominate. A nearby point is a retrieval cue; the recorded connection tells you why it is related.

Membership, source flow and the projection

H records each question's admitted allocation across destinations; overlapping allocations sum to one. Each paper receives equal total question mass, divided among its questions. A reversible flow F combines that source balance with the frozen affinities. The coarse flow is C = HᵀFH. Diffusion coordinates summarize the flow, and displayed scopes use source-balanced membership centroids. Broad scopes do not add their descendants a second time. Distortion is measured; gaps between disconnected components have no metric meaning.

The current retrieval recipe combines MiniLM question affinity (0.5), Qwen question affinity (0.3) and same-paper method-context affinity (0.2). Same-paper context is a retrieval signal, not established method applicability. Exact scientific source-object edges and admitted destination routes remain separately typed; they are not inputs to that affinity mixture. Contribution-only questions remain readable through their sources and routes without invented chart positions.

The optional native source explorer recomputes a local PCA view around a query or selected source. It is a separate mode; local axes do not construct or rename the prepared atlas. The complete accepted package and its final geometry are still being assembled. Partial rehearsals demonstrate the procedure, not complete coverage.

How scientific agents, independent challenge and steering produce the shared atlas

Two kinds of map, explicit source limits

The broad entrance uses the APS PhySH classification vocabulary. It supplies names and classification links across physics. The detailed collection has its own source-based geometry and dated coverage. The two charts have separate coordinate systems; nearby points do not establish a scientific implication.

Scientific connections retain their exact source objects, direction, conditions and review status. A method found in the same paper is labeled as context until its applicability is established. Browser title-and-alias lookup is lexical; an agent must inspect the scope before interpreting a paragraph as an existing question.

The readable atlas links the frozen source inventory and bibliographic records whose source text is unavailable. An unavailable record does not acquire invented vectors or questions.

Physics assumptions stay separate from how a record was reviewed. Explicit display annotations may move historical workflow notes into record history; the original definitions, source limits and hashes remain inspectable.

Work notes stay in the browser unless exported. A destination link identifies a recorded question without uploading those notes; sharing a full authored view is a separate explicit action. The atlas supplements independent research and reading beyond its collection.
