# Next-wave scientific instructions (draft; no requests launched)

Review the scientific questions described by the supplied members. The numerical
neighborhood is a candidate, not an admission criterion or a gold partition.
Produce a readable broad family, several distinct scoped questions, and useful
connections. A family can contain different systems, dimensions, methods and
central targets. Do not require every source to test a named Swampland conjecture.
Ordinary, adjacent and pre-Swampland work belongs where it helps answer questions.

Review every supplied nonreserved member description. The packet's immutable
`unitIndex` identifies its source unit; never edit the source statement. Report one
assessment for every index, including unclear cases. `description_checked` means
only the description was read. Use `source_excerpt_checked` only if the supplied
source excerpt for that unit was actually read. An abstract or introduction cannot
certify an announced derivation, numerical result or present-day observational fit.
The external manifest pins full numerical membership; reserved descriptions are
absent from this packet and must remain `reserved_unreviewed` in external coverage.
Do not infer what they say, create dispositions for them or claim full-paper review.

Keep notes compact. Use the packet-local indices for contributions and factors,
not repeated long source IDs. All evidence references must resolve to this stage's
packet. A fresh stage has six independent paper packets and may not cite the prior
review's source IDs, facts or membership claims as independent evidence. It may
challenge a prior scope using its own six papers; abstention is a valid result.

Propose normally 3–8 useful scoped question cards for a manageable family; fewer
are appropriate if the sources support fewer. A question is defined by its target,
physical system, premises, regime and conventions. It may be shared across routes.
Use typed `addresses`, `instantiates`, `specializes`, `method`, `boundary` or
`comparison` source contributions. These roles do not mean the entire source
statements are interchangeable. A source can address several cards. Do not
manufacture aliases or positive merge examples to fill a quota.

Reuse an existing registry question only when its scope fits. Test proposed
cross-card alias equivalence separately from source contributions. A genuine
changed-premise question needs a distinct destination and an explicit narrowing,
comparison or premise-check connection. Repeated source descriptions, nearby
vectors and common paper IDs do not prove identity. Proposed query aliases are
lexical entry points, not accepted cross-source identity decisions.

Supply 1–15 meaningful directions for the broad family and at most 15 per question.
A method can connect different systems without merging them. A failed core
membership should receive a meaningful alternate home or an explicit unresolved
placement; never discard a source merely because it lacks the core's vocabulary.
Useful negative results and contradictory claims also belong in the atlas.

Separate physical system, target, regime, method and assumptions. Cite the literal
question description or source anchor supporting a factor. Mark context-only
readings as `contextual_source_reading`; a same-paper premise is not automatically
a direct question premise. `direct_record_relation` requires a supplied exact
source-node relation connecting the relevant nodes. Do not fill unknown factors
from background knowledge. The factor dictionary expresses attributed readings,
not certified truths or automatic must-link/cannot-link rules.

Report disagreement, missing support and extraction limits. Keep the distinction
between result, conjecture, sufficient condition, necessary condition and author
claim. Preserve dimensional, boundary, regulator and normalization conventions.
Navigation proposals must not promote a new scientific implication. Existing
admitted source-object relations retain their exact endpoints, conditions and
direction in a separate source-science layer; a paper-context question route does
not change those endpoints.

For a challenge stage, independently audit all descriptions and relevant excerpts
before adopting the proposal's scope. Locate members omitted from its central
story, legitimate overlaps, overbroad merges, unnecessarily split questions and
missing alternate homes. Preserve disagreements explicitly. A verdict certifies
only this bounded review, never every source's science.

Keep the verdict on the original numerical basin separate from support for individual
proposed cards. For challenge/fresh, assess every previous question card once in
priorCardAssessments: supported as written, supported only with explicit limits,
revision required, insufficient evidence, or not evaluable from this packet.
Record whether its scope changed and which CURRENT packet units agree or disagree.
A fresh six-paper check must not certify unprovided old members. Your replacement
card is a new proposal, not independently accepted merely because you wrote it.
For each current unit, record agreement with its previous assignment when a
meaningful comparison is possible. An overall needs_revision can coexist with
agreed source-backed cards and assignments. Preserve unresolved sources in broad
or alternative homes. Downstream synthesis will reuse explicit agreement and send
changed scopes for another bounded check; it will not turn all rewrites into gold.

Return compact JSON with no indentation or repeated prose. Use short local IDs and
shared factor definitions. The hard response limit is180,000 characters. Completeness
of supplied unit assessments takes priority over rhetorical detail. A changed card
remains proposed until its scope and assignments receive an independent check.
